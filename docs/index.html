Ramps of the residual load (demand minus PV and wind) in Poland from public PSE data: hourly since 2020 and every 15 minutes since 14 June 2024. With more solar, the midday trough deepens and the evening rise steepens; the charts measure both, test a stylised IEEE RTS fleet against the ramps (IRRE) and size the storage that would keep the ramps inside their usual range.

Evening ramp and midday minimum

The evening ramp is the steepest 3-hour rise starting in the four hours before sunset (sunset computed for the centre of Poland); the lift is the rise from the day's midday minimum (10:00-16:00) to the evening peak.

What happens at the midday minimum

At the moment of each day's lowest midday residual load. Until 2023 conventional generation followed the residual load down; since 2024 it stays above it and the difference is exported.

Flexibility metric: IRRE on a test system

The insufficient ramping resource expectation (Lannoye, Flynn and O'Malley, 2012) counts the net-load ramps that exceed the upward flexibility a fleet can offer within the ramp's horizon. The fleet is the IEEE RTS-79 generating system (32 units, 3405 MW) with RTS-96 ramp rates, minimum stable outputs and start-up times, dispatched in merit order against the 2025 Polish net-load shape scaled to the RTS peak of 2850 MW. A stylised exercise: it shows at which horizons a conventional fleet runs short, not the state of the Polish system.

How much storage?

For each day of 2025 a linear programme finds the smallest storage power, and then energy (85% round trip, back to its starting charge every day), that keeps all 15-minute, 1-hour and 3-hour ramps of the residual load inside the P5-P95 or P1-P99 range of 2025. Bars show the worst day; the band is kept fixed when PV grows.
